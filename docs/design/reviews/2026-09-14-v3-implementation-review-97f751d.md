**Executive verdict**

The changes for stages 1–5 are present in the pinned main commit; the claim that all five stages are complete is false.
The existing check passes: 767 tests in 43 files, all validation commands, and the production build.
Independent probes still produce false equilibria, broken capacity limits, invalid policy runs, and a misleading “No UBI” comparison.
The faithful Korinek reproduction is strong; the dependable default and complete policy workflow are unfinished.
Keep the foundation, fix these failures, and re-review before presenting it as a dependable public decision tool.

“Met” for stage 4 does **not** mean the world simulator has passed a causal policy validation, or that every feature of the additional paper is supported. Its acceptance condition calls for classified evidence, visible discrepancies and a further model demonstrably running without evaluator/view changes. Those narrow demonstrations exist.

## What the stage 4 evidence actually establishes

**Alaska is accurately mapped for the headline employment estimand, with an important boundary.** The file records employment/population age 16+, the introduction of the dividend, July–June periods, 1982–2014 averaging, resource funding and permutation-based inference. Its +0.1 percentage-point estimate and −3.0 to +3.3 interval agree with [Table 2 of the primary paper](https://home.uchicago.edu/~j1s/Jones_Alaska_2022.pdf). The implemented calibration yields approximately **−0.724 pp**, a **−0.824 pp signed discrepancy**. It is a reproduction of the authors’ explanatory calibration compared with their estimate, not an independent prediction or a rerun of their synthetic-control estimation. [data/cases/alaska-pfd.json:48](https://github.com/sagearbor/ai-ubi-wellbeing-transition-simulator/blob/97f751d151abb961c6bcce16a7f5557a5c4dcf75/data/cases/alaska-pfd.json#L48) says that honestly. The broad top-level `policy-effect benchmark` label should always travel with that narrower classification. The world model’s transfer/employment zero is correctly identified as an absent mechanism; the other five outcomes remain visibly outside scope. Original CPS estimation and appendix replication: **not verified**.

**Gasteiger–Prettner is a fair, limited generality challenge.** It introduces a different saving/investment structure, a tax, nonlinear equilibrium and cross-entity welfare comparisons. Two Claude instances are not independent scientific authorities, but separate selection and implementation can still test software extensibility. The selection process itself is **not verified** from repository artifacts. What I verified is stronger than a commit message: its 46 embedded checks pass using the engine from `04164e8`, before `0c2a126`; its model JSON is unchanged between the port and the reviewed revision. The subsequent engine feature was therefore kept separate cleanly. However, calling gap 2 “closed” is premature because of finding 11. Generation labels and transition welfare have disclosed limitations, so this is a steady-state reproduction, not a complete transition reproduction. The 3.9% miss against the figure reading is retained in [src/core/gasteigerPrettner.test.ts:164](https://github.com/sagearbor/ai-ubi-wellbeing-transition-simulator/blob/97f751d151abb961c6bcce16a7f5557a5c4dcf75/src/core/gasteigerPrettner.test.ts#L164); that is a recorded discrepancy, not another successful published target. The [paper](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/215429/1/1693366916.pdf) does not establish that this model’s preferred tax is generally optimal.

**Korinek is faithful within the documented domain.** The JSON’s monthly production, capital-market, matching, wage and stock equations follow the [paper’s Appendix A](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cf58f84d46a4a76bf5a5b039ac695fba6b80041c.pdf), using documented algebraic elimination and unrounded calibration. The numerical checks include 169 published table cells, identities and limiting cases, rather than only model-authored assertions. I independently downloaded the authors’ explorer chunk, verified SHA-256 `a95300ab26134e7f4133d302aa220a987b95fb1e02788f37a6b6aff402ab506e`, and regenerated **all 7,150 stored values across 11 settings**. Largest difference: **4.995×10⁻¹²**. Thus the claimed oracle provenance and numerical agreement are verified. This checks implementation agreement, not empirical validity or independence of the authors’ economic assumptions. Unavailable microdata-dependent results and behavior outside the declared parameter domain: **not verified**. Keep a reproducible oracle adapter and source checksum; a checksum string alone would not have proved the original provenance.

## Numbered findings

1. **Critical — the scalar solver reports false equilibria as success.** [src/core/engine.ts:630](https://github.com/sagearbor/ai-ubi-wellbeing-transition-simulator/blob/97f751d151abb961c6bcce16a7f5557a5c4dcf75/src/core/engine.ts#L630) and [src/core/engine.ts:643](https://github.com/sagearbor/ai-ubi-wellbeing-transition-simulator/blob/97f751d151abb961c6bcce16a7f5557a5c4dcf75/src/core/engine.ts#L643). For `residual: "u"`, bracket `[0,1]`, it returns `u≈0.9999999991`, residual ≈1, `ok:true`, no diagnostic; the root is zero. For `floor(u)-0.5` on `[0,2]`, it returns success although no root exists. For `1/(u-0.3)`, the “successful” residual is approximately −5.37 billion. Lower-endpoint roots are not handled, and bracket width substitutes for residual convergence. **Fix:** check endpoint roots first; require finite evaluations and a separately scaled residual acceptance condition; distinguish a narrow bracket from a verified root. Add all three counterexamples as mandatory tests. Do not solve this by clipping the output.

## Owner decisions

**4(a) Replace the legacy default? Yes, with a corrected, explicitly provisional candidate.** The level formulation avoids accumulating a recurring wellbeing increment forever and removes the legacy crisis machinery. That is a real architectural improvement. First fix the income/log definitions, evidence labels and response review above; then promote it as “Provisional level model — evidence-informed assumptions,” retaining the legacy preset for reproducibility. Do not promote the current candidate as “validated” or “reviewed” because its graphs are smoother. Qualification of a dependable default remains a separate acceptance decision.

**4(b) Replace hand-entered country data? Yes, through a versioned migration.** I reproduce the provenance report: 126/128 GDP matches, median absolute log ratio 0.304, a typical discrepancy factor of 1.36. That is not literally a median absolute percentage error of 36%, and the mixed observation years matter. Choose a common base year and monetary convention; reconcile current-dollar country inputs with the constant-2015-dollar wellbeing fit and convert transfers consistently. Source population and GDP, retain explicit missing-data/vintage flags, and choose what “governance” means before mapping WGI into it. Do not substitute an effectiveness rank for democracy or corruption by convenience. Publish before/after results, preserve old dataset IDs for existing scenarios, then update regression snapshots after review. Preserve independent paper targets, invariants and held-out criteria. Re-pinning every expectation is not validation. Corporate revenue and adoption remain assumptions after this migration.

**4(c) Switch the world engine’s US macro path? Make the faithful port the canonical US reference, through an adapter; retire the reduced form from authoritative use.** Archive the reduced form as an explicitly illustrative legacy approximation if old links need it. Its corporation-driven adoption variable is not the paper’s `m × d`; renaming it does not make the scenarios equivalent. Preserve the paper’s time origin, monthly equations and 2030 scope, run the prehistory needed for its state, and use its labour-income and unemployment outputs once. Define the wellbeing/distribution bridge as a separately reviewed extension; do not apply the existing displacement/labour-share effects on top of the port. Unsupported transfer-to-employment effects must stay unsupported. This does not license applying the US calibration globally or forecasting arbitrarily beyond 2030, and it does not constrain the platform to Korinek: other models should continue to enter through the model interface.
